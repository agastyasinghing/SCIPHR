## 1. Executive Summary

SCIPHR is an AI-powered UFC fight prediction platform built on an XGBoost ensemble model trained on 8,000+ historical UFC fights across 5,401 features, achieving 85.7% test accuracy. It gives UFC fans, analysts, and bettors something that has never existed in one place — a genuinely intelligent prediction engine with a clean consumer interface. Not a stats dump. Not a basic win probability widget. A full intelligence experience: model predictions, public sentiment gaps, chaos scoring, narrative trap detection, AI-generated fight breakdowns powered by Claude, and shareable fight posters.

The model is the moat. The product is the vehicle. The brand is the gap between what people believe and what the data actually says.

SCIPHR's intelligence pipeline includes:

- **Aura vs Data Engine** — quantifying the gap between public hype and model probability for every fighter
- **Chaos Score** — rating how inherently volatile and unpredictable a fight is, independent of who wins
- **Narrative Trap Detector** — flagging when online discourse is emotionally strong but statistically weak
- **Upset Alert System** — surfacing when the model favors the betting underdog with meaningful confidence
- **Value Bet Engine** — comparing model probability against sportsbook implied probability to surface +EV opportunities
- **Historical Scorecard** — full transparent track record of every model call vs actual outcomes
- **Win Path Split** — heuristic breakdown of how a fight is likely to end, not just who wins
- **Public Sentiment Gap** — showing where the crowd is most wrong relative to the model
- **Sharp Money Indicator** — flagging when line movement aligns with model prediction
- **Claude AI Analysis** — deep fight breakdowns generated by Claude for every prediction
- **AI Fight Poster** — shareable Vertex AI generated fight posters with weight class theming

In v1, SCIPHR launches as a public web app — clean, fast, trustworthy, built for bettors first and fans second. In v2, it adds auth, user profiles, picks history, and a parlay builder. In v3, it becomes a premium subscription product with a paid tier unlocking the full analyst suite and real-time odds alerts.

The dual goal is to build a product that genuinely helps bettors find edge while creating a defensible, data-driven brand in a space dominated by vibes and narratives.

## 2. The Reframe: From Prediction Tool to Intelligence Platform

This distinction is the philosophical foundation of SCIPHR and should inform every product and design decision.

**What every other UFC prediction tool does:**
> "Model says Fighter A wins with 64% probability."

**What SCIPHR does:**
> "The model gives Fighter A 64% — but public sentiment is 81% in their favor, creating an perception gap of 17 points. This fight has a Chaos Score of 7.2/10 due to Fighter B's one-punch KO power and Fighter A's historically inconsistent cardio past round 2. The dominant pre-fight narrative — that Fighter A's new camp has unlocked a new version — has been flagged as a Narrative Trap: fighters who switched camps within 6 months historically underperform public expectations by 12%. The sportsbook has Fighter A at -185, implying 64.9% probability — nearly identical to the model, meaning no value exists on either side at current lines. Chaos Score suggests props over fight-to-go-the-distance may be the smart play."

The first is a coin flip machine. The second is an intelligence platform. SCIPHR is built to produce the second.

### The Four Pillars

Every feature in SCIPHR maps to one of four core pillars. If a feature doesn't serve at least one pillar, it doesn't ship.

```
PILLAR 1 — Crowd vs Model
  Where is public opinion most wrong relative to the data?
  Features: Aura vs Data Engine, Public Sentiment Gap, Upset Alert System

PILLAR 2 — Narrative vs Reality
  Where is MMA discourse emotionally strong but statistically weak?
  Features: Narrative Trap Detector, Fraud Check / Resume Inflation Index,
            Anti-Casual Mode, Aura Score

PILLAR 3 — Chaos vs Confidence
  How volatile is this fight independent of who wins?
  Features: Chaos Score, Judge Risk Score, Round Fragility Score,
            Card Temperature

PILLAR 4 — Path to Victory
  Not just who wins — how do they win?
  Features: Win Path Split, Style Collision Map, Method Probability,
            Round Fragility Score
```

### The Information Gap SCIPHR Exploits

UFC betting markets suffer from what can be called **narrative density** — the overwhelming majority of public betting is driven by stories, not statistics. Most bettors fall into one of these categories:

- Fans betting with their heart on their favorite fighter
- Casual bettors reacting to highlight reels and social media clips
- Narrative chasers following the "revenge fight" or "new camp" story
- Momentum bettors backing whoever won impressively last time
- Recency bias bettors overweighting the most recent fight

Only a small minority are genuine **data-driven bettors** — people with probabilistic reasoning, historical context, and systematic models. This structural inefficiency is what SCIPHR is built to exploit.

The model doesn't have emotions. It doesn't care about the narrative. It doesn't know Conor McGregor is a superstar. It sees 5,401 features and outputs a probability. That gap between what the model sees and what the crowd believes is where edge lives.

### Why The Window Exists Now

UFC betting is structurally early in its data-driven evolution. Most participants are casual. Most public picks content is narrative-driven. Most "prediction" products are glorified stat displays. A well-built intelligent system with a strong brand can occupy a differentiated position — but this window compresses over time as more sophisticated operators enter the space. The correct time to build SCIPHR is now.

## 3. Problem Statement

UFC fans and bettors have access to more fight data than ever before — but almost no tools that intelligently synthesize it into actionable predictions. The existing landscape is either:

**Too simple:** Basic win probability calculators that output a single percentage with no context, no confidence calibration, no method breakdown, no narrative analysis.

**Too complex:** Raw stat sites like UFC Stats that require the user to do all the analysis themselves — hours of work most bettors won't do.

**Too narrative-driven:** ESPN, Bleacher Report, and MMA podcasts that are excellent at storytelling but systematically biased toward recency, star power, and emotional resonance over historical data.

**The result:** Bettors make decisions based on incomplete information, emotional narratives, and crowd sentiment — exactly the conditions that create exploitable market inefficiencies.

### The Three Core Problems

**Problem 1 — The Narrative Trap**
MMA discourse is dominated by lazy but compelling narratives: new camp transformations, revenge fight motivation, "wanting it more," aura and momentum. These narratives feel predictive but historically they are not. No existing tool explicitly measures and flags the gap between narrative strength and statistical backing.

**Problem 2 — The Crowd Illusion**
Public betting percentages move lines. When 80% of bettors back Fighter A, the sportsbook adjusts the line to balance their book — creating value on Fighter B regardless of true probability. Most bettors have no visibility into where the crowd is wrong relative to a data-driven model. SCIPHR makes this gap explicit and actionable.

**Problem 3 — The Chaos Blindspot**
Traditional prediction models output a winner probability but ignore fight volatility. A 65% favorite in a high-chaos fight (explosive fighters, short notice, weight miss, cardio questions) is fundamentally different from a 65% favorite in a low-chaos technical matchup. Most tools treat them identically. SCIPHR doesn't.

## 9. System Architecture Overview

SCIPHR is organized into four layers: Data Layer, Intelligence Engine, API Layer, and Frontend. Each layer has explicit contracts, is independently deployable, and communicates via clean REST interfaces.

```
       │                  │                  │
```

### Event Flow — Full Prediction Happy Path

1. User selects Fighter A and Fighter B in Simple Mode
2. Frontend calls `api.js` → `POST /predict`
3. Backend validates: are both fighters in dataset? are they different fighters?
4. Lookup fighter feature vectors from PostgreSQL by fighter name
5. XGBoost model runs `predict_proba()` on combined feature vector
6. Intelligence Engine runs in parallel:
   - **Aura Engine** pulls public sentiment score, calculates perception gap
   - **Chaos Scorer** evaluates fight volatility from fighter attributes
   - **Narrative Detector** scans active narratives, flags traps
   - **Value Bet Engine** fetches current odds, calculates implied probability vs model
   - **Win Path Heuristics** derives method probabilities from career stats
7. Claude API called for AI analysis generation (with template fallback if API fails)
8. Full response assembled and returned to frontend
9. Frontend renders Simple Mode (winner, confidence, perception gap badge, upset alert if applicable)
10. User can expand to Analyst Mode for full intelligence breakdown
11. Prediction logged to `predictions_log` table with timestamp, fighters, result, confidence, odds at time of pick
12. User can generate AI poster, download, or share to Twitter/X

### Latency Budget

| Step | Target |
|---|---|
| Fighter lookup (PostgreSQL, indexed) | <50ms |
| Model inference (predict_proba) | <200ms |
| Intelligence Engine (parallel) | <300ms |
| Claude AI analysis | <3000ms (async, non-blocking) |
| Total response (excl. Claude) | <600ms |
| Total response (incl. Claude) | <4000ms |

Claude analysis loads asynchronously — the prediction result renders immediately, the AI analysis panel shows a loading state and populates when ready. Users are never blocked waiting for Claude.

**Mirage Meter (Resume Inflation Index):**
```python
def mirage_meter(fighter_stats: dict) -> float:
    """
    Score 0-10: how inflated is this fighter's record relative to opposition quality?
    High score = impressive record, weak opponents = potential mirage
    Low score = record backed by genuine quality wins
    """
    # Components:
    # - Average opponent win rate at time of fight
```

### 10.5 Aura vs Data Engine

**Purpose:** The centerpiece feature of SCIPHR's brand. Quantifies the gap between public hype and model reality for every fighter in every fight.

**Aura Score derivation (v1 — heuristic, no live API):**

For v1, aura score is derived from measurable proxies within the dataset and manual signals rather than live social scraping (which requires API access and adds complexity):

```python
def calculate_aura_score(fighter_stats: dict, fight_context: dict) -> float:
    """
    Aura Score: 0-100 representing public hype level
    High score = heavily hyped fighter
    Low score = underrated / quiet fighter
    """
    components = {
        # Proxy for star power: how long has this fighter been active at top level?
        'tenure_factor': normalize(fighter_stats['years_active'], 0, 15) * 0.20,
        
        # Finish rate as a proxy for highlight reel appeal
        'finish_appeal': fighter_stats['finish_rate'] * 0.25,
        
        # Recent win streak — public loves momentum
        'momentum': normalize(fighter_stats['current_win_streak'], 0, 10) * 0.20,
        
        # Championship history — title fights attract casual attention
        'title_factor': (1.0 if fighter_stats['has_title_history'] else 0.0) * 0.20,
        
        # Betting favorite status — public backs favorites, favorites are hyped
        'favorite_factor': normalize(fighter_stats['pct_fights_as_favorite'], 0, 1) * 0.15,
    }
    return clamp(sum(components.values()) * 100, 0, 100)

def aura_gap(fighter: str, model_probability: float, 
             aura_score: float) -> AuraGapResult:
    """
    The gap between what people think and what the model says.
    Positive gap = fighter is overhyped relative to model
    Negative gap = fighter is underrated relative to model

    aura_implied_probability = aura_score / 100
    gap = aura_implied_probability - model_probability

        aura_score=aura_score,
        model_probability=model_probability,
        gap=gap,
        direction="OVERHYPED" if gap > 0.10 else "UNDERRATED" if gap < -0.10 else "ALIGNED",
        magnitude="LARGE" if abs(gap) > 0.20 else "MODERATE" if abs(gap) > 0.10 else "SMALL"
    )
```

### 10.6 Chaos Score

**Purpose:** Rate how inherently volatile and unpredictable a fight is, independent of who the model favors. High chaos = higher variance = adjust bet sizing, look at props.

```python
def chaos_score(fighter1_stats: dict, fighter2_stats: dict, 
                fight_context: dict) -> ChaosResult:
    """
    Chaos Score: 0-10
    0-3: Predictable — technical fight, low variance
    4-6: Mixed — some chaos factors present
    7-8: High chaos — expect the unexpected
    9-10: Extreme chaos — anything can happen
    """
    factors = {
        # KO power of either fighter (one-punch danger raises chaos)
        'ko_power': max(
            fighter1_stats['ko_rate'], 
            fighter2_stats['ko_rate']
        ) * 2.0,
        
        # Cardio consistency (fighters who fade raise chaos)
        'cardio_variance': (
            fighter1_stats['late_round_finish_rate'] + 
            fighter2_stats['late_round_finish_rate']
        ) * 1.5,
        
        # Sample size (fewer fights = less predictable)
        'sample_size_penalty': max(0, (20 - min(
            fighter1_stats['total_fights'],
            fighter2_stats['total_fights']
        )) / 20) * 1.5,
        
        # Style variance (brawlers raise chaos vs technical fighters)
        'style_clash': (
            1.0 if fight_context['style_matchup'] == 'BRAWLER_VS_BRAWLER' 
            else 0.5 if fight_context['style_matchup'] == 'MIXED'
            else 0.2
        ),
        
        # Short notice flag
        'short_notice': 1.5 if fight_context.get('short_notice') else 0,
        
        # Weight miss flag
        'weight_miss': 1.0 if fight_context.get('weight_miss') else 0,
        
        # Days since last fight (long layoff raises chaos)
        'layoff': normalize(
            max(fight_context.get('fighter1_days_since_last_fight', 0),
                fight_context.get('fighter2_days_since_last_fight', 0)),
            0, 730
        ) * 1.0,
    }
    
    raw_score = sum(factors.values())
    normalized = clamp(raw_score, 0, 10)
    
    return ChaosResult(
        score=normalized,
        tier=(
            "EXTREME" if normalized >= 9 else
            "HIGH" if normalized >= 7 else
            "MIXED" if normalized >= 4 else
            "LOW"
        ),
        contributing_factors=factors,
        betting_implication=(
            "High variance — consider props over fight-going-to-distance" if normalized >= 7
            else "Moderate variance — standard prediction confidence applies" if normalized >= 4
            else "Low variance — high-confidence technical matchup"
        )
    )
```

```python
def upset_alert(model_probability: float, betting_odds: dict) -> UpsetResult:
    """
    Upset Watch triggers when:
    1. Model gives the betting underdog >= 45% probability
    2. The underdog is priced as a +150 or greater underdog on sportsbooks
    This means the market underestimates them by a meaningful amount.
    """
    if not betting_odds:
        # No odds data: use model confidence threshold only
        is_upset_alert = model_probability >= 0.45 and model_probability < 0.55
        return UpsetResult(is_alert=is_upset_alert, 
                          reason="MODEL_CONFIDENCE_NEAR_EVEN" if is_upset_alert else None)
    
    favorite = max(betting_odds, key=lambda x: betting_odds[x]['implied_prob'])
    underdog = min(betting_odds, key=lambda x: betting_odds[x]['implied_prob'])
    
    underdog_model_prob = model_probability if underdog == "fighter2" else 1 - model_probability
    underdog_implied_prob = betting_odds[underdog]['implied_prob']
    underdog_american_odds = betting_odds[underdog]['american_odds']
    
    # Upset Watch: model gives underdog significantly more than market implies
    edge = underdog_model_prob - underdog_implied_prob

    return UpsetResult(
        is_alert=is_alert,
        underdog_name=underdog,
        model_probability=underdog_model_prob,
        implied_probability=underdog_implied_prob,
        edge=edge,
        american_odds=underdog_american_odds,
        payout_multiplier=underdog_american_odds / 100 + 1
    )
```

### 10.9 Value Bet Engine

**Purpose:** Compare SCIPHR's model probability against sportsbook implied probability to surface +EV opportunities. The core feature for the sharp bettor persona.

**Odds data source:** The Odds API (free tier — 500 requests/month, sufficient for v1 fight week usage)

```python
from odds_api import TheOddsAPI

class ValueBetEngine:
    def __init__(self, api_key: str):
        self.api = TheOddsAPI(api_key)
    
    def get_fight_odds(self, fighter1: str, fighter2: str) -> dict:
        """Fetch current odds for this matchup from The Odds API."""
        # MMA markets on The Odds API
        events = self.api.get_odds(
            sport='mma_mixed_martial_arts',
            regions='us',
            markets='h2h'
        )
        # Match fighter names to event
        return self._match_fighters(events, fighter1, fighter2)
    
    def calculate_edge(self, model_prob: float, 
                       implied_prob: float) -> ValueBetResult:
        """
        Edge = model probability - implied probability
        Positive edge = model thinks fighter is more likely to win than market implies
        Edge > 5% = EDGE DETECTED (display badge)
        Edge > 10% = STRONG EDGE (highlight prominently)
        Edge < -5% = FADE OPPORTUNITY (contrarian signal)
        """
        edge = model_prob - implied_prob
        
        if edge > 0.10:
            signal = "STRONG_EDGE"
        elif edge > 0.05:
            signal = "EDGE_DETECTED"
        elif edge < -0.05:
            signal = "FADE_OPPORTUNITY"
        else:
            signal = "NO_EDGE"
        
        # Kelly criterion for suggested bet sizing
        if edge > 0:
            decimal_odds = 1 / implied_prob
            kelly_fraction = (model_prob * decimal_odds - 1) / (decimal_odds - 1)
            suggested_kelly = kelly_fraction * 0.25  # quarter Kelly, conservative
        else:
            suggested_kelly = 0
        
        return ValueBetResult(
            edge=edge,
            signal=signal,
            suggested_kelly_fraction=suggested_kelly,
            model_probability=model_prob,
            implied_probability=implied_prob
        )
```

**The DNA dimensions (7 axes):**

```python
DNA_DIMENSIONS = [
    "patience_vs_chaos",        # 0 = calculated/patient, 10 = chaotic/unpredictable
    "pressure_vs_counter",      # 0 = pure counter-fighter, 10 = relentless frontfoot
    "finish_urgency",           # 0 = happy to go to decision, 10 = always hunting finish
    "collapse_risk",            # 0 = iron chin/gas tank, 10 = fades or gets hurt badly
    "early_vs_late_danger",     # 0 = dangerous late, 10 = most dangerous early
    "stylistic_rigidity",       # 0 = highly adaptable, 10 = fights one way regardless
    "scramble_comfort",         # 0 = avoids chaos positions, 10 = thrives in scrambles
]

def calculate_fight_dna(fighter_stats: dict) -> FightDNA:
    """
    Derive each DNA dimension from real statistical proxies in the dataset.
    All values normalized to 0-10 scale.
    """
    return FightDNA(
        patience_vs_chaos=derive_chaos_tendency(fighter_stats),
        pressure_vs_counter=derive_pressure_index(fighter_stats),
        finish_urgency=derive_finish_urgency(fighter_stats),
        collapse_risk=derive_collapse_risk(fighter_stats),
        early_vs_late_danger=derive_timing_danger(fighter_stats),
        stylistic_rigidity=derive_style_rigidity(fighter_stats),
        scramble_comfort=derive_scramble_index(fighter_stats),
    )

def dna_collision(fighter1_dna: FightDNA, fighter2_dna: FightDNA) -> DNACollision:
    """
    Compare two DNA profiles. Identify:
    - MIRROR: both fighters similar on dimension (neutral)
    - CLASH: opposite extremes (creates volatility)
    - EXPLOIT: one fighter's strength meets other's weakness (creates edge)
    """
    collisions = []
    for dim in DNA_DIMENSIONS:
        f1_val = getattr(fighter1_dna, dim)
        f2_val = getattr(fighter2_dna, dim)
        delta = abs(f1_val - f2_val)
        
        if delta < 2:
            collision_type = "MIRROR"
        elif delta > 6:
            collision_type = "CLASH"
        else:
            collision_type = "NEUTRAL"
        
        collisions.append(DNADimension(
            dimension=dim,
            fighter1_value=f1_val,
            fighter2_value=f2_val,
            collision_type=collision_type,
            delta=delta
        ))
    
    # Overall DNA clash score: how much do these styles create volatility?
    clash_score = sum(c.delta for c in collisions if c.collision_type == "CLASH") / len(collisions)
    
    return DNACollision(dimensions=collisions, clash_score=clash_score)
```

### 10.14 False Consensus Detector

**Purpose:** Flag fights where the model sees a near coinflip that public discourse is treating as obvious. This is one of SCIPHR's sharpest brand voice moments — the system calling out manufactured certainty before it embarrasses the crowd.

**The core insight:** A fight priced at -250/-200 (implying ~70%/67% win probability) where the model gives 54/46 is not a confident fight — it's a disguised coinflip. The public thinks they know. They don't. SCIPHR says so.

```python
def false_consensus_check(model_prob: float, implied_prob: float,
                           public_sentiment_pct: float) -> FalseConsensusResult:
    """
    False Consensus triggers when:
    1. Model says fight is close (45-55% range for either fighter)
    2. Public/market treats it as obvious (>65% implied or sentiment)
    
    The gap between "close fight" and "obvious fight" is the False Consensus.
    """
    model_is_close = 0.42 <= model_prob <= 0.58
    market_is_confident = implied_prob >= 0.65 or public_sentiment_pct >= 0.70
    
    if model_is_close and market_is_confident:
        consensus_gap = max(implied_prob, public_sentiment_pct) - model_prob
        return FalseConsensusResult(
            is_false_consensus=True,
            label=_assign_label(consensus_gap),
            model_probability=model_prob,
            market_implied=implied_prob,
            public_sentiment=public_sentiment_pct,
            consensus_gap=consensus_gap,
            display_message=_generate_message(consensus_gap)
        )
    
    return FalseConsensusResult(is_false_consensus=False)

def _assign_label(gap: float) -> str:
    if gap >= 0.25:
        return "INVISIBLE COINFLIP"      # Market wildly overconfident
    elif gap >= 0.15:
        return "FAKE MISMATCH"           # Labeled a blowout, model says close
    elif gap >= 0.08:
        return "PUBLIC CERTAINTY TRAP"   # Crowd more sure than data warrants
    return "MILD OVERCONFIDENCE"

def _generate_message(gap: float) -> str:
    if gap >= 0.25:
        return "The public sees a walkover. The model sees a coinflip. Someone is very wrong."
    elif gap >= 0.15:
        return "This fight is being sold as obvious. The data disagrees significantly."
    return "Market confidence exceeds what the model justifies. Proceed with caution."
```

**Display:** A bold banner on the fight card page — not subtle. "FALSE CONSENSUS DETECTED" with the label (Invisible Coinflip, Fake Mismatch, etc.) and one sentence of context. This is SCIPHR speaking with its fullest brand voice.

```python
                              fighter1_name: str, fighter2_name: str) -> NightmareResult:

    for archetype_id, archetype in NIGHTMARE_ARCHETYPES.items():
        # Check if fighter2 is the stylistic nightmare for fighter1
        f1_is_victim = _matches_profile(fighter1_dna, archetype['victim_profile'])
        f2_is_predator = _matches_profile(fighter2_dna, archetype['predator_profile'])
        
        if f1_is_victim and f2_is_predator:
            detected.append(NightmareMatchup(
                archetype=archetype_id,
                label=archetype['label'],
                victim=fighter1_name,
                predator=fighter2_name,
                explanation=archetype['explanation'].format(
                    predator=fighter2_name, victim=fighter1_name
                )
            ))
        
        # Check reverse
        f2_is_victim = _matches_profile(fighter2_dna, archetype['victim_profile'])
        f1_is_predator = _matches_profile(fighter1_dna, archetype['predator_profile'])
        
        if f2_is_victim and f1_is_predator:
            detected.append(NightmareMatchup(
                archetype=archetype_id,
                label=archetype['label'],
                victim=fighter2_name,
                predator=fighter1_name,
                explanation=archetype['explanation'].format(
                    predator=fighter1_name, victim=fighter2_name
                )
            ))
    
    return NightmareResult(
        matchups=detected,
        has_nightmare=len(detected) > 0,
        severity="SEVERE" if len(detected) >= 2 else "MODERATE" if detected else "NONE"
    )
```

```python
import pandas as pd
from sqlalchemy import create_engine, text
import os

def migrate():
    csv_path = "data/golden_full_converted.csv"
    db_url = os.environ["DATABASE_URL"]
    
    print(f"Loading CSV ({os.path.getsize(csv_path) / 1e6:.0f}MB)...")
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    
    print(f"Rows: {len(df):,} | Columns: {len(df.columns):,}")
    
    engine = create_engine(db_url)
    
    print("Migrating to PostgreSQL...")
    df.to_sql(
        name="fights",
        con=engine,
        if_exists="replace",
        index=True,
        index_label="id",
        chunksize=1000,
        method="multi"

    print("Adding indexes...")
    with engine.connect() as conn:
        conn.execute(text("CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS idx_fights_fighter1 ON fights(fighter1_name)"))
        conn.execute(text("CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS idx_fights_fighter2 ON fights(fighter2_name)"))
        conn.execute(text("CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS idx_fights_date ON fights(fight_date DESC)"))
        conn.commit()
    
    print("Migration complete.")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    migrate()
```

## 19. v2 Roadmap

### User Layer
Add Google OAuth or email/password auth. User profiles with picks history. "My record vs SCIPHR" dashboard — did you follow the model's picks? How did you do? Personal accuracy tracking.

### Parlay Builder
Select 2-5 fights, SCIPHR calculates the combined model probability vs sportsbook parlay odds. Displays +EV or -EV verdict. Quarter-Kelly suggested stake. This is the most addictive engagement feature — users spend 30+ minutes building and evaluating parlays.

### Automated Sentiment (Perception Gap v2)
Replace heuristic perception gap scores with live Reddit API pulls from the fight announcement thread. NLP extracts public sentiment automatically. Narrative Trap triggers from real discourse, not manually tagged signals.

### Line Movement Tracker
Full fight week odds chart for every fight on the card. Opening line → current line → percentage moved. Sharp Echo powered by real opening line data.

### Card Temperature Auto-Calculation
Auto-derived from fight data, not manually assigned. Chaos scores compute for all fights on a card automatically as fights are added to the event.

### Shame Score (Gamified)
Post-fight: show which consensus takes aged worst. "Fighter A was 79% public — they lost." Auto-populated from predictions_log after each event. Full receipt theater — what SCIPHR got right, what it underestimated, what narrative proved false, whether the upset alert was justified, biggest discourse miss of the week. Viral weekly content format.

### Automated Narrative Detection
NLP pipeline that automatically scans MMA media (Bloody Elbow, MMA Fighting, ESPN MMA) for narrative signals. No more manual tagging in the events JSON.

### Fight Clock — Timeline Simulator
A fight page where users scrub across a hypothetical fight timeline: Round 1 early / Round 1 late / Round 2 / Round 3+ / Championship rounds. At each phase, SCIPHR updates who has edge and why — derived from Fight DNA collision data, fighter cardio profiles, and historical round-by-round finish rates. Instead of one static pick, it shows: early danger belongs to Fighter A, if it crosses 7:30 cage time edge swings to Fighter B, decision equity favors Fighter B, chaos spike in first 4 minutes. Makes the analysis cinematic and interactive. Requires Fight DNA (v1) as a dependency.

### Victory Anatomy — "If He Wins It Looks Like This"
For every fighter, a scenario card showing what their victory path looks like: early pressure and damage accumulation, control time and clinch attrition, sudden counter KO in transition. Not just who wins — what movie has to happen for them to win. Shareable as a standalone card. Built from Fight DNA + historical win method data.

### Legacy Swing Meter
A feature that shows how much the result of a specific fight changes each fighter's narrative arc: title legitimacy jump, contender resurrection, fraud-check risk, old guard collapse, star coronation potential. Displayed as a 0-10 Legacy Swing score with a one-line consequence for each outcome. Casual fan gold, extremely viral for big fights. Example: "This fight has a 9.1/10 Legacy Swing. If Fighter B wins, the division narrative resets completely."

### Discourse Fever
Separate from Card Temperature — measures how insane the online conversation is around a specific fight rather than how volatile the fight itself is. Components: polarizing score, confidence asymmetry, casual invasion level, narrative saturation, meme density proxy. A low-chaos fight can have extreme Discourse Fever (heavily debated technical matchup). A high-chaos fight can have low Discourse Fever (casual fans not paying attention). The contrast is the insight.

### Casual Trap Mode
A toggle showing what a casual fan would probably pick based on knockout highlights, physique bias, star power, recency bias, and crowd narrative. Displayed next to SCIPHR's actual call. Funny, shareable, and validates the Perception Gap concept visually. "The crowd says X. SCIPHR says Y. Someone is wrong."

### Division Web
A dynamic graph for each division showing who beats who stylistically, the title threat map, dark horse paths, gatekeeper pockets, and bottleneck contenders. Makes SCIPHR feel like a strategic war room, not a picks site. Built on Fight DNA data accumulated during v1. Each node is a fighter, edges show stylistic matchup advantage directions.

### Fighter Archetype / Villain-Hero Index
Based on Fight DNA, career history, and public perception data, each fighter is assigned a primary archetype: Aura Merchant, Chaos Goblin, Silent Killer, Discourse Demon, Beloved Technician, Fraud Magnet, Card Breaker. Displayed on fighter profiles with a short descriptor. Half serious, half playful — gives the platform personality and makes fighter browsing addictive.

### "What The Crowd Will Say" — Pre-emptive Receipt
Before the fight: SCIPHR predicts what the post-fight discourse will look like for each outcome. If favorite wins clean: "Levels discourse — expected." If underdog wins ugly: "Fluke discourse spike forecast." If it goes to decision: "Robbery claims likelihood: HIGH." If prospect loses: "Fraud allegations forecast: SEVERE." People share this before the fight then revisit it after. Sticky, viral, and a natural Shame Score setup.

### Fan Alignment Profile
Users take a short interaction quiz or enough usage generates a profile automatically. SCIPHR classifies them: Data Mercenary, Chaos Hunter, Hype Believer, Underdog Romantic, Technical Purist, Violence Addict, Narrative Chaser. Platform adapts how it frames fights for them — Data Mercenary gets value bet emphasis, Chaos Hunter gets Chaos Score front and center. Sticky personalization without requiring complex auth infrastructure.

Retrain XGBoost with method of victory as an additional target variable. Kill Chain becomes model-powered rather than heuristic. Quarterly retraining schedule as new fight data accumulates.

Affiliate partnerships with DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM. "Bet this pick" button that deeplinks to the sportsbook with the fighter pre-selected. Revenue share on deposits from SCIPHR referrals.

### Dataset Licensing
After 12+ months of operation, SCIPHR's predictions_log is a labeled dataset of model calls vs reality. License this to academic researchers and quant sports betting operations.

### Alternate Universe Engine
Run a matchup in different contexts: 3 rounds instead of 5, small cage vs big cage, short notice vs full camp, championship rounds only, post-prime vs prime version. Clearly labeled as fan simulation mode — not predictive claims. Extremely fun for group watch settings and content creation. Built on Fight DNA and Fight Clock infrastructure from v2.

### Walkout Aura Score
A semi-serious, high-engagement side feature: walkout song intimidation rating, stare-down composure, entrance energy, crowd reaction spike, visual menace factor. Not a prediction input — purely fan-facing. Displayed as a before-fight panel. People would share these constantly. Great social content clip format for creators.

### Soundtrack Mode
For huge cards (UFC 300-tier events): a full presentation mode with dramatic visuals, countdowns, fight archetype cards, division stakes, and model edge reveals. Almost like a war-room presentation meets ESPN intro package. Killer for creators, group watch settings, and podcasts. Premium v3 feature.

### Aesthetic Fight Score — "Most Beautiful Fight on the Card"
A score for technical intrigue, style contrast, transition richness, momentum swing potential, and violence elegance. Not the best betting spot — the most artistically interesting fight. Great for non-betting fans. Validates watching for the craft of combat sports, not just money.

### Rivalry & Mythology Tracker
Track recurring themes between fighters and divisions over time: the Uncrowned King, the Gatekeeper Curse, the Wrestler Wall, the Revenge Spiral, the Title Eliminator Graveyard. SCIPHR starts to feel like it understands the symbolic side of combat sports, not just the data. Requires predictions_log history built up over v1/v2 operation.
